A tankless water heater works on a simple idea: instead of keeping a full tank of water hot around the clock, it heats water only while a tap is open. Flow through the unit trips a sensor, a powerful burner or electric element fires, and the water is heated as it passes through a compact heat exchanger. The upside is significant: no standby heat loss, hot water that does not run out during back-to-back showers, a wall-mounted unit that frees up floor space, and a service life that often runs close to two decades with maintenance. For plenty of Temple, TX households, that makes tankless a smart upgrade.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We size by flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver noticeably less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, which often means a larger gas line or a meter check. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need well over 100 amps, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

Every tankless install from Temple Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, a clean gas connection with a sediment trap, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is the main enemy of a tankless heat exchanger. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Limited floor space in a closet, garage, or utility room
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household's morning routine
  • Construction or remodeling that opens walls and simplifies the install
  • A growing household or new bathroom adding hot water demand
  • An addition or guest suite that is a long run from the main heater
  • Wanting a unit that can last up to twice as long as a typical tank
  • An aging tank that has started to rust, rumble, or leak
  • Energy bills that reflect a tank heating water around the clock

The Benefits of Professional Tankless Water Heater Installation

The equipment is only half the job. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so gas line sizing, meter capacity, and pressure under full load all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are dictated by the manufacturer and local code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Get any of these wrong, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

A professional install protects both performance and safety. We start with a real demand assessment, confirm the gas and electrical supply, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, then a test with several fixtures running. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The goal is the right water heater, not just a tankless sale.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Write down any error codes and call early rather than resetting repeatedly
  • A setting near 120F limits scalding and reduces scale formation
  • Consider a water softener or scale-prevention filter if your water is hard
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year
  • Keep the air intake and vent termination clear of snow, leaves, and debris
  • Do not cut power to a unit in a cold space, since its freeze protection needs electricity
  • Plan on yearly descaling through the service valves, sooner if your water is hard
