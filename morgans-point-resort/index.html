Keeping Your Morgan's Point Resort Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

In Morgan's Point Resort, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
