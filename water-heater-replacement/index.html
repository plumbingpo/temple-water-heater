Every tank water heater has a limited life, and the majority reach the end somewhere between year eight and year twelve. How long yours lasts in Temple comes down to water hardness, water pressure, and whether the tank was ever flushed or its anode rod replaced. The signs tend to repeat: rust-colored hot water, popping and rumbling from sediment, a shower that turns cold sooner every month, and, most urgently, water around the base of the tank. That last one settles the question. When the inner tank rusts through, the leak cannot be patched, and the only real fix is a new heater.

A replacement is the perfect moment to fix whatever the last heater got wrong. Temple Water Heater sizes the new unit to your household, not to whatever was printed on the old tank. For tanks, that means looking at the first-hour rating, the amount of hot water the heater can deliver in a busy hour, rather than tank gallons alone. For tankless units, it means sizing to flow rate in gallons per minute and to the temperature rise your incoming water needs in winter. You get a plain, no-pressure rundown of gas versus electric, tank versus tankless, and standard versus heat pump, and we check whether your gas line, venting, and electrical service can support the choice before you commit.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. The old heater is drained and carried out, the new one is set level, and fresh supply connectors and a working shutoff go in. Gas units get a leak-tested connection with a sediment trap and venting that meets the manufacturer's spec. The relief valve discharge is routed where it belongs, and we add an expansion tank, drain pan, or earthquake straps wherever local code requires them, including the permit if one applies. Signs You Need Water Heater Replacement in Temple

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes

Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. A correctly rated relief valve has to be piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models pile on more: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
